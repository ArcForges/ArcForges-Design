## Context

On 2026-10-18 the product owner asked for a study of how far ArcForges can evolve beyond process execution and resource control while staying faithful to its philosophy, "Your machine. Your rules." The request was for research, architecture, an independent review and roadmap and documentation changes only, with no implementation. It authorized recording validated, non-implementation proposals in the authoritative design and roadmap without another confirmation (request §10).

The study produced a research record with a lab register, three concepts and their judgements, a synthesis, an independent review round, a re-review and, on 2026-10-19, an independent confirmation pass. The review history stays in the study's working files and is not part of the Design. Its outcome is recorded in two Design-then-Plan PR pairs:
- **Pair 1** corrected overstatements in today's Design (merged 2026-10-19, Design PR #8): escape detection, same-user tampering with ArcForges' containers, routing out of a client's own sandbox, UAC on administrator accounts, the cloud command key's reach, and related threat rows T-22 … T-27 and boundaries B0 and B11.
- **Pair 2** records the direction: this decision, the amendments it lists, [chapter 17](../architecture/17-delegated-execution.md), the [research record](../research/2026-10-18-delegated-execution/README.md), and then the roadmap rows in the Plan.

The study found the direction viable and coherent under three conditions: it stays bounded by the design rules M1–M5 (principle 8), with "concept" defined and every concept showing two uses; truthfulness is treated as the product, so attainable values are never shown as realized and nothing falls back silently; and confinement is promoted only through the lab gates, one backend at a time, after the owner's decisions. No confinement lab has run, so every new capability is at most "feasible but needs a prototype" (E4) until its lab runs.

This decision holds **principles only**. The concepts, the strength ladder, the inheritance rules and the contract sketch are a **proposed** model in chapter 17. Each element of that model is accepted by an ADR amendment when the first roadmap row that needs it is promoted.

**The product owner's objection of 2026-10-19, recorded verbatim** (the relevant passages of the request of 2026-10-19 and of its scope correction of the same day; the full texts stay in the study's working files):

> I need to correct a fundamental misunderstanding in your previous long-term evolution study.

> I am NOT requiring ArcForges to build a fully isolated graphical desktop or comprehensively isolate every GUI interaction channel.
> The absence of complete GUI isolation must not, by itself, disqualify a native GUI application from receiving meaningful process-level filesystem, network, registry, and resource restrictions.

> Native GUI applications must be legitimate first-class candidates for genuine OS-enforced process-level sandboxing, subject to actual technical feasibility and compatibility.

> However, do not overcorrect by pretending that GUI-related channels are irrelevant to security.

> ArcForges is a cross-platform, local-first application and workload control infrastructure that unifies resource coordination, process lifecycle management, policy enforcement, verifiable access restrictions, and operational diagnostics.
> The operating system remains responsible for actual scheduling and security enforcement.
> ArcForges provides a coherent management, coordination, policy, and reporting layer over the mechanisms that each OS genuinely supports.

> This means that the machine owner determines what applications may access, how much resource capacity managed workloads may consume, when work should be admitted or deferred, and which policies are actually enforced.
> Do not turn this into an unrealistic promise of controlling every process on every operating system.

> These applications should not have to implement an ArcForges SDK merely to benefit from all resource controls.

> Accepting a product goal does not mean claiming the mechanism already works.
> Roadmap inclusion does not authorize implementation.

> ArcForges is not intended to become a graphical display isolation system.
> Nor is it intended to remain exclusively an AI-agent process supervisor.

From the scope correction of 2026-10-19:

> I am NOT asking ArcForges to research or implement GUI display isolation, window isolation, keyboard/mouse isolation, screen protection, or a secure graphical desktop.
> When I say "GUI application sandboxing," I mean OS-enforced restrictions on the application's processes, including:
>
> * CPU and memory resource control.
> * Filesystem access restrictions.
> * Network access restrictions.
> * Windows Registry restrictions.
> * Process and subprocess management.
> * Storage budgets where genuinely enforceable.
> * Application compatibility and resource-limit diagnostics.

> A GUI application is simply another native application workload with a graphical interface.
> For example, I want ArcForges to manage a compatible WeChat, ArcSlate, or game process without modifying the application's own GUI or requiring the application to integrate with an SDK.

> However, recording a GUI-related security limitation is not authorization to expand the product into GUI isolation, secure display surfaces, anti-overlay systems, or separate-device approval mechanisms.

> The purpose is a practical application and workload control platform, NOT a secure desktop environment.
> This remains roadmap research only. Do not implement anything or alter the current task sequence.

The objection amends principle 1's framing, boundary 3.4 and chapter 17's GUI conclusions (the exclusion of GUI applications from confined profiles, "UNCONFINED only" for portable GUI applications, and EX-19). Chapter 17 §17 decision 22 records it as the answer. The study that followed (research, a completeness critique, gap-fill research, three concepts, three independent judgements and a synthesis, on 2026-10-19) found the corrected direction viable under the same conditions as before, M1–M5 above all, with **exactly one new concept** (the cooperative resource-grant family); its review history stays in its working files. No lab has run, so every new capability is at most E4.

## Decision

AD-0017's study-derived principles are recorded as accepted design rules. **The product owner may object to any of them.** An objection is recorded verbatim in this decision and changes the principle by amendment. The question is also listed in chapter 17's owner decisions table (§17, decision 22). **On 2026-10-19 the owner objected** (recorded verbatim in the Context): principle 1 and boundary 3.4 are amended, principles 2, 4, 5, 8, 9 and 11 are extended, and principles 12 and 13 and boundaries 3.11 … 3.13 are added, each with its basis and effective clause (amended 2026-10-19).

Each principle states its **basis** (text quoted from the owner's request, an existing accepted Design position, or a study-validated design rule recorded under request §10) and its **effective** clause: either it is true of release 1 today, or it takes effect when a named roadmap row is promoted.

1. **The framing, and the bounded reading of "Your machine. Your rules."** (amended 2026-10-19). As a Design description, never a product name, ArcForges' long-term direction is, in the product owner's words, *"a cross-platform, local-first application and workload control infrastructure that unifies resource coordination, process lifecycle management, policy enforcement, verifiable access restrictions, and operational diagnostics."* *"The operating system remains responsible for actual scheduling and security enforcement. ArcForges provides a coherent management, coordination, policy, and reporting layer over the mechanisms that each OS genuinely supports."* For every workload it launches or the owner adopts (AI agents and their commands, MCP servers and tools, builds, native desktop applications first-party or closed-source, media production, IDEs, compatible games and their launchers, long-running local services), ArcForges will state what that work may use, realize it with operating-system mechanisms, read the realization back, refuse when a requirement cannot be met, supervise what it launched or the owner adopted, own or honestly label the lifecycle of what it brought onto the machine, record what happened, diagnose whether a limit the owner set caused a failure, and let the owner revoke it from any surface, a phone included. **"With verified contracts"** is the property every value has: requested, realized, read back, or refused. Work the owner hands to AI agents and tools, *the local authority for delegated execution* ("authority" in the sense of [AD-0004](AD-0004-contracts-and-state.md): the only writer of execution state), is one family of these workloads, the first family, the one release 1 is built for; it no longer defines the product. Everything ArcForges did not launch and the owner did not adopt, it observes and labels. ArcForges is not a graphical display isolation system or a secure desktop (boundary 3.11). The phrase is read in a bounded way, after the owner's own reading, "the machine owner determines what applications may access, how much resource capacity managed workloads may consume, when work should be admitted or deferred, and which policies are actually enforced":
   - **"Your rules":** the owner writes one policy per application or workload, and every launch or adoption ArcForges performs either realizes it or is refused with a reason. "Actually enforced" is the value read back from the operating system, never the number typed.
   - **"Your machine":** ArcForges runs as the user and never elevates at run time ([AD-0005](AD-0005-resource-enforcement.md), Process model), so it cannot control everything on the machine. It observes what a standard user can see and labels it; it controls what it launched or what the owner explicitly adopted (and, from EX-15's holds stage and only by the owner's decision, what the owner held); it never claims more than the operating system enforces. "Local workload control infrastructure" is used only with the qualifier "launched or adopted".
   - **Basis:** the product owner's words of 2026-10-19 (the Context), quoted; the bounded reading is a study-validated design rule; the phrase "Your machine. Your rules." is the owner's (the ArcForges repository README).
   - **Effective:** now, as a description. "Your rules" holds today for resource requirements only (delivered: `require_enforcement` and refusal, R-RES-008). For per-application limits, access, lifecycle and diagnosis it is direction, effective per row as each is promoted.
   - **Superseded 2026-10-19** (kept as history; its successor is the text above): "As a Design description, never a product name, ArcForges' long-term direction is *the local authority for delegated execution, with verified contracts*. "Authority" has the sense of [AD-0004](AD-0004-contracts-and-state.md): the only writer of execution state. When the owner hands work to a delegate (an AI agent, an MCP server, a tool, a script, a portable application, a LAN peer), ArcForges will state what that work may use, realize it with operating-system mechanisms, read the realization back, refuse when a requirement cannot be met, own or honestly label the lifecycle of what the delegate brought onto the machine, record what happened, and let the owner revoke it from any surface, a phone included. Everything ArcForges did not launch, it observes and labels." Its "Your rules" bullet read: "the owner writes policy once, and every launch ArcForges performs either obeys it or is refused with a reason." Its Basis read: "a study-validated design rule; the phrase itself is the owner's (the ArcForges repository README)."
2. **The wording rules**, for every surface:
   - never "sandboxed", "contained", "protected" or "secure" alone, and never a single yes/no bit;
   - never "authority" alone: it is paired with the end-state sentence of the [product definition](../product/product-definition.md) §2;
   - a tier name is a basis field, never a guarantee;
   - "controls" always carries "what it launched or what the owner explicitly adopted"; from EX-15's holds stage, and only after the owner's decision on the product definition §7 holds amendment ([17](../architecture/17-delegated-execution.md) §17, decision 17), it also carries "or held";
   - a persistence hold is "held", never "blocked".
   - **Basis:** a study-validated design rule.
   - **Effective (the five rules above):** now, for every surface text, release-1 text included. The 2026-10-19 corrections already follow them.
   - **Added 2026-10-19** (rules (a) to (e), with their own basis and effective clause): (a) a limit is never shown as a bare number: it carries its verb, its measure and its enforcer ("Hard limit at 256 MiB of committed memory: may end the app"); (b) never "limited to" for a soft limit ("slowed above, not limited to"); (c) "Enforced" never for GUARDRAIL, HINT, OBSERVED or a coordination-only grant, and owner-directed limits never "tamper-proof", "locked" or "cannot be undone"; (d) never "confirmed" for a diagnosis below CONFIRMED; (e) a GUI application is never called unconfinable for having windows, and a display or IPC route is named as the launcher cell it is.
   - **Basis of (a) … (e):** the owner's words of 2026-10-19 ("A requested limit must have an explicit enforcement status, mechanism, scope, and explanation." and "Do not promise arbitrary resource limits merely because a UI allows the user to enter a number."), with study-validated wording rules.
   - **Effective of (a) … (e):** at the promotion of the row that introduces what each governs: (a), (b), (c) and (e) when RM-16 or RM-18 is promoted, whichever comes first; (d) when RM-17 is promoted. **Release-1 surfaces keep their delivered wording;** making any of them effective now would change delivered release-1 texts and the acceptance of planned surface tasks, a release-1 matter outside this direction ([18](../architecture/18-application-workload-control.md) §19, decision 5).
3. **The boundaries.**
   1. **Control** covers only what ArcForges launched or the owner explicitly adopted. A future adoption kind, persistence holds, would be recorded, reversible and done through the operating system's own mechanism, and it needs its own owner decision (EX-15). **Machine-wide services, drivers, installers and processes of other accounts are never controlled; an application installed machine-wide but launched or adopted as the owner is a managed process** (amended 2026-10-19; superseded: "Machine-wide software is never controlled.").
   2. **ArcForges never performs an OS-level change silently; an optional privileged step is either the owner's own action or, where [AD-0011](AD-0011-identity-and-login.md) §6 applies, an explicit OS prompt with a journal entry and exact removal.** AD-0011 §6 says that the optional privileged features, "when built, … install through explicit OS prompts (UAC, sudo)", labelled as OS-level changes. This boundary aligns with it, so the designed optional privileged features AR-16 (machine-wide service mode) and A-L02 (Linux I/O control) stay as [AD-0005](AD-0005-resource-enforcement.md) designs them. At run time ArcForges never elevates.
   3. **No kernel component, driver, system extension or code injection.**
   4. **Not a package manager, a registry proxy, an application store, or a replacement for the desktop's launcher, menu or default-application settings. ArcForges launches applications only as tasks it supervises.** A small store of tool versions acquired on explicit request is the most it would own, and even that is weighed against delegating to an established manager (EX-13 stage b). It is never an apt or rpm frontend. Whether ArcForges may route the owner's ordinary starts (an ArcForges shortcut or desktop entry, a launch-at-node-start setting, owner hand-off rules, holding a vendor's autostart) is the owner's decision, recorded here verbatim when given ([18](../architecture/18-application-workload-control.md) §19, decision 10). (Amended 2026-10-19; superseded: "**Not a package manager, registry proxy or application launcher.**")
   5. **Not a VM or container manager.** STRONG backends are orchestrated OS facilities or established projects, each accounted as one consumer like the WSL VM ([AD-0005](AD-0005-resource-enforcement.md), WSL2 model). ArcForges never manages images, registries, volumes or networks (EX-18).
   6. **Not an endpoint-security agent (EDR).** Observation stays at standard-user privilege by default; privileged observation is an opt-in the owner grants (EX-20).
   7. **Local and LAN stay free** ([AD-0015](AD-0015-direction-editions-roadmap.md) §2), every capability of this direction included. No local default may depend in practice on a Pro remote decision.
   8. **The execution targets are Windows, Linux and macOS; Android, iOS and the Web are control clients.** macOS execution takes effect with RM-15 (principle 10).
   9. **ArcForges never changes machine-wide security policy**, except as an optional privileged step under boundary 2.
   10. **ArcForges' own privileges stay minimal:** no administrator rights at run time and no new OS group membership, except opt-in integrations (EX-20), each reported as a privilege increase of the owner's account.
   11. **Not a graphical display isolation system or a secure desktop** (added 2026-10-19). ArcForges builds no display, window, input or clipboard isolation, no secure display surface and no anti-overlay system, and adds no separate-device approval mechanism as a remedy for a GUI or display exposure. A display or IPC channel enters the model only where it decides a process-level guarantee: as a launcher cell that caps `holds_against`, as a named gap of the dimension it carries data out of, or as a disclosed dependency on a vendor-serviced boundary ([17](../architecture/17-delegated-execution.md) §3.2). Release 1's opt-in remote approval path is unchanged, and EX-23's future is the owner's decision ([18](../architecture/18-application-workload-control.md) §19, decision 7).
   12. **Not a system optimizer or a "game booster"** (added 2026-10-19). No global deprioritization of processes ArcForges does not manage, no core parking, timer-resolution, power-plan or memory-trimming features, no frame-rate claims; hints are never called limits.
   13. **No catalogue of access profiles or limits for third-party software, and no cloud compatibility database** (added 2026-10-19). Owner-written profiles, first-party capability lists and local observations only.
   - **Basis:** boundary 8 is the request, section 3: "The execution targets are: Windows, Linux, and macOS. Android, iOS, and the Web are control clients, not local application sandbox or execution targets. That distinction is final." Boundary 7 is [AD-0015](AD-0015-direction-editions-roadmap.md) §2 (existing). Boundary 2 is [AD-0011](AD-0011-identity-and-login.md) §6 (existing), with the study's journal and exact-removal clause. The others are study-validated design rules. Boundary 11's display, window, input and secure-desktop exclusions are the owner's words of 2026-10-19 ("ArcForges is not intended to become a graphical display isolation system." and "I am NOT asking ArcForges to research or implement GUI display isolation, window isolation, keyboard/mouse isolation, screen protection, or a secure graphical desktop."); its separate-device clause follows the owner's "recording a GUI-related security limitation is not authorization to expand the product into GUI isolation, secure display surfaces, anti-overlay systems, or separate-device approval mechanisms." Boundaries 12 and 13 are study-validated design rules after the owner's "Avoid turning ArcForges into a collection of unrelated utilities, an application store, a full virtual machine manager, or a replacement for the operating system." The rewording of boundaries 1 and 4 is recorded on the owner's words of 2026-10-19: integration level 3, "Launching and supervising an application under ArcForges.", and "I want ArcForges to manage a compatible WeChat, ArcSlate, or game process without modifying the application's own GUI or requiring the application to integrate with an SDK."
   - **Effective:** now: each describes what release 1 already does or never does. The parts that name future work take effect with their rows: persistence holds (boundary 1) at EX-15's holds stage; the journal entry of boundary 2 at EX-08; the opt-in privilege increases of boundary 10 at EX-20; macOS execution (boundary 8) at RM-15. Boundary 11 now, for what ArcForges builds; it changes nothing in release 1. Boundaries 12 and 13 now, as descriptions of what release 1 already does not do; the wording clause of boundary 12 ("hints are never called limits") with principle 2 (a). The rewording of boundaries 1 and 4 now (release 1 already launches commands as tasks); the forms of launch indirection only by the owner's answer.
4. **Require → refuse, with no silent fallback.** A task's requirements are REQUIRED or PREFERRED. When a REQUIRED requirement cannot be met, ArcForges refuses, with a reason and before any workload code runs; it never falls back to a weaker mode, and it never merely warns. A PREFERRED shortfall is recorded and shown on every surface.
   - **Basis:** the request, section 7: "If a requested security property cannot be enforced, do not silently downgrade to a weaker execution mode while continuing to claim the stronger guarantee."; R-RES-008 (existing, delivered for resources).
   - **Effective:** now for resource requirements, in R-RES-008's terms: a task whose policy requires enforcement is refused, and other tasks run unenforced only where policy allows it, labelled as such. The REQUIRED/PREFERRED split for access dimensions, with a PREFERRED shortfall shown on every surface, takes effect when EX-27 stage b is promoted with the first confined backend (amended 2026-10-19; previously "EX-07 stage b", whose stages continue as EX-27 since the product owner's decision of 2026-10-19).
   - **Extended 2026-10-19:** the rule covers every per-limit owner request of an application (a REQUIRED verb the host cannot realize refuses before the application runs), adoption with limits, and the `min_holds_against` requirement. **Basis:** the owner's words of 2026-10-19 ("A requested limit must have an explicit enforcement status, mechanism, scope, and explanation."), with a study-validated design rule. **Effective:** per-limit requests at RM-18 and RM-16; access dimensions and `min_holds_against` at EX-27 stage b.
5. **The tiered threat scope.** The threat scope is stated per workload state, one row each: unconfined (today, and the default); confined, per realized dimension; STRONG backend, per backend; privileged installers and system software. The unconfined row is the statement of [11](../architecture/11-security-privacy.md) §2, unchanged and not weakened: any same-user process can reach the node, and malware running as the user is out of scope. The table is in [17](../architecture/17-delegated-execution.md) §4.1.
   - **Basis:** [11](../architecture/11-security-privacy.md) §2 (existing) for the unconfined row; a study-validated design rule for the others.
   - **Effective:** the unconfined row now; each other row when its row is promoted (EX-10, EX-11 or EX-22 for the confined row; EX-18 for STRONG backends); the privileged-installer row: its structural-prevention statement now ([AD-0005](AD-0005-resource-enforcement.md), Process model), its detection statement when EX-15 is promoted.
   - **Extended 2026-10-19:** the table gains the row **"Confined, on a shared display (per display system)"**: A1–A2 for every realized dimension while any launcher cell, the display's included, is open or unmeasured; A1–A3 only where every cell of that realization is measured denied; the read exposure stated in one fixed sentence per platform ([17](../architecture/17-delegated-execution.md) §4.1). **Two promise levels:** Contain (A1, A2) is the default promise of every control, GUI and headless alike; Distrust (A3) is the owner's `min_holds_against` requirement, which refuses when unmet. **Basis:** a study-validated design rule. **Effective:** the row with the first GUI stage of EX-10 or EX-11, as this principle's other rows take effect with their rows; the promise levels with `min_holds_against` at EX-27 stage b.
6. **The scope's invariant, now-part:** "The node never extends the privileges of any caller."
   - **Basis:** a study-validated design rule, true of release 1 ([11](../architecture/11-security-privacy.md) §2; R-SEC-001; a caller's own sandbox is not seen or preserved: principle 7, T-24).
   - **Effective:** now.
7. **The scope's invariant, direction-part:** "When confined attempts exist, the node never relaxes the confinement of a caller it confined. Today it cannot see a client's own sandbox, and commands routed through it run outside that sandbox (T-24, B11)."
   - **Basis:** a study-validated design rule.
   - **Effective:** when EX-09 is promoted.
8. **The definition of "concept" and the design rules M1–M5.** A **concept** is a new persistent entity, a new state machine, or a new contract service or message family. Roles of the one binary, enum values and record kinds inside an existing family are not concepts.
   - **M1. One execution lifecycle.** Everything that runs is a task with attempts (SM-TASK, SM-ATTEMPT): an installer run, an MCP server, a confined agent command, an inference server, a desktop application, a game launcher, a model server, and later the AI client itself (amended 2026-10-19). They differ in profile, kind (`COMMAND`, `SERVICE`, `ADOPTED`), placement and the records they feed, never in a new runtime.
   - **M2. A dimension exists only if a grant or a resource request can target it on at least one OS.** Facts nobody grants or requests are basis attributes of a reported value. A seccomp rule that implements a denied grant is a policy filter and counts toward its dimension; surface hardening stays basis only.
   - **M3. Lifecycle and isolation stay independent facts.** Lifecycle facts never raise an isolation strength, and isolation facts never raise a lifecycle level, except through the weakest-run rule of uninstall. The intended couplings are one-directional and recorded ([17](../architecture/17-delegated-execution.md) §2.1).
   - **M4. The node is the only writer.** Grants are node-held records referenced by ID, never bearer tokens.
   - **M5. No new concept without two uses.** Every concept names two capabilities it serves and why no existing primitive can carry it. A new theme needs the same test.
   - **Basis:** a study-validated design rule; it answers the request's demand for a coherent model rather than a collection of features (request §1 and §6).
   - **Effective:** now, as a rule for every future Design change. It changes nothing in release 1.
   - **The concept count of 2026-10-19:** chapter 17's five proposed concepts plus **one**, the cooperative resource-grant family (message family and service, with its grant lifecycle), whose uses are an application's own running process, a confined cooperator's attempt endpoint (EX-09) and EX-26's designated declarations; the Installation enters earlier with a stated subset; everything else of the application model is a field, an enum value, a record kind or an amendment of a delivered state machine ([18](../architecture/18-application-workload-control.md) §7, §9). Theme 7 passes M5: its three rows share the one per-application record and the one scheduler.
9. **The mission-level rule.** Where a verified contract and completion time conflict, the owner's profile decides per task. ArcForges never trades a REQUIRED dimension for speed. A CONFINED **default** for an origin may cost at most **X %** in median completion time on the adapters' reference workloads; the study proposes X = 10, and **the owner sets X**. This keeps [AD-0015](AD-0015-direction-editions-roadmap.md) §1's objective, useful completion time and reliability, in force.
   - **Basis:** a study-validated design rule; X by the owner ([17](../architecture/17-delegated-execution.md) §17, decision 5).
   - **Effective:** "never trades a REQUIRED dimension for speed": now, for resource requirements. The X % ceiling: when the first CONFINED default for an origin is proposed.
   - **Extended 2026-10-19:** an application-class default (a limit verb or a confinement proposed for a class) may be proposed only within the owner's interactive cost ceilings (`Y-start`, `Y-input`, `Y-frame`, `Y-hang`) on the class's reference applications; the owner sets them, as X; the X % completion-time rule stays for batch work ([18](../architecture/18-application-workload-control.md) §15). **Basis:** a study-validated design rule. **Effective:** when the first application-class default is proposed.
10. **The platform roles.** Windows, Linux and macOS execute; Android, iOS and the Web are control clients.
    - **Basis:** the request, section 3: "The execution targets are: Windows, Linux, and macOS." and "That distinction is final."
    - **Effective:** now, as recorded direction. macOS execution takes effect when RM-15 is promoted ([AD-0002](AD-0002-platform-scope.md), amendment (1)); until then release-1 scope is unchanged.
11. **"Release 1 is unchanged; confinement is optional, and UNCONFINED stays the default until the compatibility, overhead and editions gates pass."** Amended 2026-10-19: **owner-directed limits on an application exist only by the owner's per-application decision; no limits and UNCONFINED stay every application's default.**
    - **Basis:** a study-validated design rule; the request, section 11: "This work is limited to research, architecture, review, and roadmap/documentation changes." The amendment's basis: a study-validated design rule, after the owner's "This means that the machine owner determines what applications may access, how much resource capacity managed workloads may consume, when work should be admitted or deferred, and which policies are actually enforced."
    - **Effective:** now.
12. **Independence, and two resource models on one engine** (added 2026-10-19). Monitoring, lifecycle ownership, resource enforcement, access confinement and cooperation are independently expressible and composable, subject to two physical couplings: access confinement needs a launch, and an enforced grant needs a container ([18](../architecture/18-application-workload-control.md) §4.1). Resource management never requires a sandbox, a sandbox never requires cooperation, and cooperation never exceeds the owner's maximums (`granted ≤ min(request, per-application maximum, aggregate cap left, B)`, the authorization bound). Every grant, resize or hold also passes the one admission engine's rule: memory is admitted only if, afterwards, the accounted memory of running attempts and outstanding guest grants is at most `B_mem`, computed with every live hold, the new one included, in `K_mem` (so `avail_mem ≥ 0`, and a hold is counted once), otherwise the answer is DEFERRED; CPU is clamped to `B_cpu` within the CPU overcommit bound ([03](../architecture/03-resource-control.md) §10). Owner-directed enforcement needs no cooperation from the application. Both resource models use the one admission engine and the one per-application record; there is no second scheduler.
    - **Basis:** the owner's words of 2026-10-19 for the independence of monitoring, lifecycle ownership, resource enforcement and security confinement ("They should be independently expressible and composable."); for cooperation and sandboxing not requiring each other ("There should be no requirement that an application must be fully sandboxed merely to participate in resource coordination. Equally, an application should not need to participate in resource coordination to benefit from a compatible sandbox."); for owner-directed enforcement and resource controls not requiring cooperation or an SDK ("Owner-directed enforcement: ArcForges applies supported restrictions without cooperation from the managed application." and "These applications should not have to implement an ArcForges SDK merely to benefit from all resource controls."); and for the owner's maximums ("The application must never be able to bypass the owner's maximum limits merely by asking for more resources."). A study-validated design rule ([18](../architecture/18-application-workload-control.md) §4.3 and §9; gates G45, G46) for listing cooperation as a fifth composable facet, the clamp formula's terms, the admission rule, and one admission engine and one per-application record with no second scheduler.
    - **Effective:** now, as a design rule; each mechanism with its row (RM-18, EX-25, RM-09).
13. **Evidence-bounded diagnosis** (added 2026-10-19). A failure diagnosis never claims more than its evidence: a limit's behaviour sets the highest verdict it can reach; an application's own report (content it sends over an endpoint, writes to its output or writes to a log) never confirms; an exit status read through a held handle is a program-chosen signature that counts only together with a kernel refusal record at the enforced value and machine headroom; missing evidence yields "cannot tell", never "unrelated".
    - **Basis:** the owner's words of 2026-10-19 ("It should distinguish a confirmed limit-related failure from a suspected correlation or an unrelated crash."), with a study-validated design rule ([07](../architecture/07-records-diagnosis-memory.md) §4).
    - **Effective:** when RM-17 is promoted.
